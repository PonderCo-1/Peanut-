For: The Peanut Advice That Backfired

Sources

The clue: two countries
  1. 1
    Early consumption of peanuts in infancy is associated with a low prevalence of peanut allergyJournal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, 2008 (Du Toit et al.). Supports scenes 4-5 and 12-16: guidelines in the UK, Australia and North America advised avoiding peanut in infancy while allergy kept rising; peanut allergy 1.85% among Jewish children in the UK vs 0.17% in Israel, not explained by atopy, social class or genetic background; Israeli infants ate peanut early and often, UK infants a median of zero.
The trials
  1. 1
    Randomized trial of peanut consumption in infants at risk for peanut allergyNew England Journal of Medicine, 2015 (Du Toit et al.). Supports scenes 23-29: 640 infants aged 4 to 11 months with severe eczema, egg allergy or both; avoidance vs consumption until age 5; 13.7% vs 1.9% and 35.3% vs 10.6% peanut allergy; more protective IgG4 in eaters, more IgE in avoiders.
  2. 2
    Effect of Avoidance on Peanut Allergy after Early Peanut ConsumptionNew England Journal of Medicine, 2016 (Du Toit et al.). Supports scenes 30-31: after 12 months of avoidance, allergy in the early eaters rose only from 3.6% to 4.8% (not significant).
  3. 3
    Follow-up to Adolescence after Early Peanut Introduction for Allergy PreventionNEJM Evidence, 2024 (Du Toit et al.). Supports scenes 32-33: at age 12, peanut allergy 15.4% in the original avoiders vs 4.4% in the early eaters, even after years of eating or avoiding peanut as desired.
How allergy works, and why it might start
  1. 1
    IgE and mast cells in allergic diseaseNature Medicine, 2012 (Galli and Tsai). Supports scenes 6-10: IgE antibodies and mast cells drive anaphylaxis and other acute allergic reactions.
  2. 2
    Epicutaneous sensitization in the development of food allergy: What is the evidence and how can this be prevented?Allergy, 2020 (Brough et al.). Supports scenes 18-22: sensitization through damaged skin as one half of the dual-allergen exposure hypothesis; the link between eczema and food allergy.
How common and how dangerous
  1. 1
    US prevalence of self-reported peanut, tree nut, and sesame allergy: 11-year follow-upJournal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, 2010 (Sicherer et al.). Supports scenes 2-3: peanut and tree nut allergy is the leading cause of fatal allergic reactions in the US; self-reported peanut allergy in children rose from 0.4% (1997) to 1.4% (2008).
The new advice and safety
  1. 1
    Peanut Allergy Prevention: Guidelines from the NIAIDAmerican Family Physician, July 2017 (summary of the US addendum guidelines). Supports scenes 34-35: severe eczema or egg allergy, peanut foods at 4 to 6 months after IgE or skin prick testing; mild to moderate eczema, around 6 months at home; others, with other age-appropriate solid foods.
  2. 2
    Guidelines for Clinicians and Patients for Diagnosis and Management of Food Allergy: Addendum Guidelines for the Prevention of Peanut Allergy (clinician summary)National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, 2017. Supports scenes 34-35: the three guidelines for age-appropriate peanut-containing food.
  3. 3
    When can I start giving my baby peanut butter?HealthyChildren.org (pediatric parent guidance). Supports scene 35: whole peanuts are a choking hazard; thinned peanut butter mixed into food is a safe way to introduce peanut.
Added in this version: the real-world check
  1. 1
    Guidelines for Early Food Introduction and Patterns of Food AllergyPediatrics, 2025 (Gabryszewski et al.). Supports scene 34: in a multistate primary care records network, peanut IgE-mediated food allergy in children aged 0-3 fell from 0.79% to 0.45% after the addendum guidelines (hazard ratio 0.55). An observational study: a pattern, not proof.

A note on wording

The "tiny flags" for IgE antibodies are an analogy. The comparison between the two countries is an observational study based on questionnaires: it showed a pattern, not proof, which is why the video moves on to the randomized trial. The skin-versus-gut idea is a scientific hypothesis, so the video says "the idea is", "may" and "if that is true". The trials studied babies at high risk (severe eczema, egg allergy or both). Early introduction is about prevention, not treatment of an existing allergy. Whole peanuts are a choking hazard for young children. This video is for information only and is not medical advice; before giving peanut to a baby with eczema or other allergies, talk to your doctor.
